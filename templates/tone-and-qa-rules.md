# Tone and QA Rules (Global, applies to all clients)

## Service Definitions

These two services are often confused. Never describe them the same way or use overlapping language between them:

- Tree Removal: cutting down a tree section by section
- Tree Cutting: felling a tree in one piece

When writing about either service, state the actual method (section by section versus felling in one piece) rather than a generic phrase like "taking the tree down," so the two services stay clearly distinct.

## Voice

* Confident, professional, reassuring, like a seasoned local expert
* No exaggeration or hype
* No superlative or ranking claims: never use "best," "top," "number one," "leading," "greatest," or similar, for any client, even if a client requests it
* No unverified guarantees ("lifetime warranty," "guaranteed same-day") unless that exact claim is listed in the client's profile file as something they actually offer
* Emphasize experience, safety, and reliability instead of hype

## Reading Level and Sentence Structure

* Grade 8 to 9 reading level (Hemingway app standard)
* Average sentence length: 10 to 14 words. Never below 6, never above 18
* Active voice, present tense
* Avoid single-clause choppy fragments strung together
* Mostly single-clause or simple two-clause sentences with simple conjunctions (and, but, so, when)
* Avoid complex subordinate clauses and passive phrasing
* One comma per sentence is the default. If a sentence needs two commas, cut the middle clause or split into two sentences

## Perspective

* Second person ("you," "your property")
* Aim for "you" or "your" in roughly 40 to 50 percent of sentences, not every sentence
* Vary how "you" is used: as subject ("You can protect your home..."), as object ("Our team gives you..."), as possessive ("Your lawn stays..."), or implied through a command ("Call our local team today...")
* Keep "we" to a minimum: no more than about 20 percent of sentences should use "we." Shift the subject instead: the business name ("Fant's Tree Service lowers each limb..."), "our crew," "our arborists," "the team," or the reader ("You get..."). Where a template rule calls for "we or our" (such as FAQ second sentences), prefer "our" or the business name

## Formatting Rules

* Convert [h1], [h2], [h3] into Markdown # ## ### in sequential order. No HTML tags, no leftover brackets
* Back-to-back sentences or paragraphs must not start with the same word
* H3 heading text itself can repeat words across a section (e.g., "Tree Trimming," "Tree Removal," "Tree Cutting" are all fine next to each other, since these are fixed service, USP, or process names)
* The body text (description) under consecutive H3s must not start with the same word (hard rule)
* No em dashes, en dashes, or ellipses (unless directly quoting)
* No slashes in place of "or" or "and"
* No ampersands in body text
* No asterisks for emphasis
* No more than one parenthetical phrase per sentence
* Hyphens only for compound adjectives before a noun
* Quotation marks only for direct quotes or technical terms

## Word Choice

* No comma-plus-ing constructions (", ensuring," ", protecting," ", making")
* Avoid adverbs where possible, especially "very," "super," "way," "actually"
* Use strong verbs and compound adjectives instead of adverb-plus-weak-verb combinations
* Avoid fluff phrases: "we are passionate," "we care deeply," "you deserve," "when it comes to"
* Avoid redundant and filler words
* Cut the middle clause or split the sentence rather than stacking modifiers

## Required Terms (Every Page)

Work these terms into every page naturally. Never force them into a sentence where they read awkwardly, and never stack two of them in one sentence.

* "Arborist" or "arborists": at least 2 times
* Each of these at least once: "tree experts," "tree care professionals," "local," "experienced," "tree surgeons"

Claims rule: these terms must not create a claim the client profile doesn't support. If a client has no arborist on staff (or the profile says not to claim one), use "arborist" in a general sense instead of "our arborist," for example "an arborist can tell whether..." or "the kind of pruning an arborist recommends." Never pair "arborist" with "certified" unless the client profile confirms a certification.

## Heading Rules

* H3 headings themselves may repeat a word (service names like "Tree Trimming" and "Tree Removal" are fine next to each other)
* The description text under consecutive H3s must not start with the same word
* H3 descriptions under the same H2 should stay similar in length for layout alignment
* Customized local H2 heading must not repeat a heading already used for the same client in its last 5 pages

## Banned Words List (Global)

This list applies to every client. Never use these words or phrases, in any form (including variations like "crafting" for "craft," "navigating" for "navigate"). Each client profile may add client-specific banned words on top of this list.

Amplify, adaptive, assist, augment, automate, bespoke, bold, boost, but also, commend, contextual, craft, crafting, curate, curated, captivate, captivates, catalyze, comprehensive, cutting-edge, daunting, delve, dive deep, disrupt, discover, deploy, efficiently, elevate, embark, empower, endeavor, enhance, ensure, entrust, esteemed, ever-changing, ever-evolving, financial future, foster, future, game-changer, groundbreaking, heart, harness, intricate, landscape, master, merely, meticulous, meticulously, navigate, navigating, not only, optimize, optimizing, peace of mind, passionate, persistent, precise, precision, prompt, proven, realm, relentless, relentlessly, revolutionize, rise above, rigorous, robust, savvy, seasoned, seek, seeking, sharp, skilled, skyrocket, smart, straightforward, swift, swiftly, systematic, systematically, streamline, tailored, tapestry, top-tier, transform, underpins, underscore, unlock, unparalleled, unveil, uncover, utmost, best, top, number one, leading, greatest, deserve, when it comes to

Also avoid fluff and unnecessary adjectives or adverbs generally. Write in plain, everyday, conversational language, like talking to the reader directly, not like marketing copy.

## Thin-Content Filter (Competitor Research)

Word count is a priority signal, not a hard skip rule. Check pages at or above 300 words first, since they are more likely to have useful material, but do not skip shorter pages automatically.

For every competitor page, regardless of length, test each heading or phrase before using it:

* Specific, not generic: is it genuinely tied to this location (a specific landmark, local problem, local regulation, local tree species), or could it apply to any city?
* Adds something new: does it say something not already covered in the template or client profile, or is it the same idea in different words?
* Not boilerplate: is it actual information, or marketing fluff ("best service," "trusted name")?

Only use a heading or phrase that passes all three tests. Never copy a competitor's phrase verbatim. Adapt it into original wording that fits this client's voice.

## QA Checklist (Run Before Finalizing Any Page)

1. Word counts per section match the template and population tier
2. No banned words present (global list plus client-specific list)
3. No superlative or guarantee claims outside what the client profile confirms
4. No two consecutive H3 descriptions (body text, not the heading itself) share a starting word
5. No two consecutive sentences or paragraphs share a starting word
6. Business name used consistently in the sections that call for it (Why Choose Us intro, Working Process intro)
7. Headings follow proper H1 to H3 Markdown structure, no brackets left in text
8. No em dashes, ellipses, slashes-as-conjunctions, or ampersands in body text
9. "You/your" appears in roughly 40 to 50 percent of sentences, not forced into every sentence
10. FAQ answers give a direct answer first, then a second sentence naming the business's own action ("our" or the business name preferred over "we"), per the FAQ rules in the page template
11. "We" appears in no more than about 20 percent of sentences
12. Required Terms present: "arborist" at least 2 times, and "tree experts," "tree care professionals," "local," "experienced," and "tree surgeons" at least once each, with no unsupported arborist claims
