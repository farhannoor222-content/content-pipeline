# GBP Post Templates

Use this file for every Google Business Profile post. Apply the shared rules below to every post type unless a type's own section overrides it. Pull business details from the client's about.md, customer-persona.md, and pipeline-notes.md, same as location pages. Apply the Banned Words list here together with the list in tone-and-qa-rules.md.

Before writing, check the client's "GBP Post Tracker" in pipeline-notes.md for which structure, opening, or CTA was used recently. Rotate away from recent choices wherever this file says to. After finishing, add a row to that tracker.

## Shared Rules (all post types except Review Posts)

### Word Count
70 to 100 words per post. Never go over 100.

### Emoji
No emoji anywhere in the post body. Emoji appears only in the CTA line, as each type's section specifies.

### Bullet Lists
Use a checkmark before each bullet item. Always add one short lead-in sentence before the list, naming what the list covers. Keep each bullet short enough that it won't line-wrap awkwardly when copy-pasted. Only one bullet list per post, and across a business's last 4 regular posts, only 1 should use a bullet list.

### Sentence and Voice Rules
- No question or interrogative sentences anywhere in the post
- Sentence length: 8 to 12 words typically, never below 5, never above 20
- Mostly simple sentences, with occasional compound or complex sentences for rhythm
- Never leave a 3 to 4 word sentence standing alone, combine it with the next one
- The first paragraph must be informative, not just a hook
- Address the reader as "you"
- Write in first person plural: "we," "our," never "I"
- No passive voice ("get removed," "is handled," "are cleaned"), state who does the action
- Never make an impersonal claim ("Free quotes help...") attribute it to the business ("Our free quotes help...")
- Never use the word "certified"
- Mention the primary service area exactly once per post
- Say how or with what the business does something (name the method, equipment, or tool), not just that it's done "safely" or with "modern tools"
- When citing local knowledge, name the specific detail (species, landmark, regulation), never just claim "local expertise" in the abstract
- Never repeat the same local detail twice within one post
- Don't open consecutive sentences with generic subjects like "Crews," "Written estimates," "Cleanup includes" use "Our crew," "Our free estimates," "We also offer cleanup"

### Punctuation
- No em dashes
- No comma-plus-ing constructions ("Trimming carefully, we...")
- City and state: "Leander, TX," with the comma after the state when a clause follows, never "Leander TX" or a dangling "Leander, TX" with no following comma

### Bad vs. Good Writing
Bad: "Snow and ice weigh branches down. Limbs snap and fall unexpectedly."
Good: "Snow and ice weigh branches down, while limbs can snap and fall unexpectedly."

### Heading
Every post type needs a heading or title above the post, except Review Posts, which need none.

### Banned Words
(in addition to the global list in tone-and-qa-rules.md)
Typically, Amplify, adaptive, assist, augment, automate, bespoke, bold, boost, but also, commend, contextual, craft, crafting, curate, captivate, captivates, catalyze, comprehensive, cutting-edge, daunting, delve, dive deep, disrupt, discover, deploy, efficiently, elevate, embark, empower, endeavor, enhance, ensure, entrust, esteemed, ever-changing, ever-evolving, financial future, foster, future, game-changer, groundbreaking, heart, harness, intricate, landscape, master, merely, meticulous, meticulously, navigate, navigating, not only, optimize, optimizing, peace of mind, persistent, precise, precision, prompt, proven, realm, relentless, relentlessly, revolutionize, rise above, rigorous, robust, savvy, seasoned, seek, seeking, sharp, skilled, skyrocket, smart, straightforward, swift, swiftly, systematic, systematically, streamline, tailored, tapestry, top-tier, transform, underpins, underscore, unlock, unparalleled, unveil, uncover, utmost

## Post Type 1: Regular Post

Used for general tree-care topics that aren't an offer, review, entity, or cost-range post: warning signs, timing questions, maintenance advice, process questions, and similar.

Three structures exist below. Pick one per post based on the topic, and rotate so consecutive posts for the same business don't feel identical.

### Choosing a structure
- Structure B (bullet-list): use when the topic itself asks for signs, problems, or a list-style answer (e.g., "Warning Signs a Tree Is About to Fall," "Signs Your Tree Needs Removal")
- Structure A or Structure C: use for every other regular topic. Alternate between A and C; never repeat the same structure as the business's immediately preceding regular post

### Structure A
- Para 1 (20 words): a direct answer if the title needs one. State the problem and how it shows up on the property. Assure the business, named, can help.
- Para 2 (30 words): explain the solution in detail. Promote the service. Bold the location name. Name the actual method or equipment used, tied to safe or professional handling.
- Para 3 (15 words, one sentence): start with a relevant CTA emoji and verb (Book, Call, or Schedule). Reassure on reliability, fast response, convenience. No phone numbers.

### Structure B (bullet-list)
- Para 1 (20 words): describe the problem clearly, how homeowners notice it, why it's a concern. Bold the location name once.
- Para 2 (30 to 35 words): a direct answer in the first sentence, one transition sentence leading into a list, then 4 to 5 bullets, each 3 to 4 words, each starting with a checkmark.
- Para 3 (20 words): start with "Call" plus a phone emoji, mention the business name once, promote its experience, promise safe professional service. No phone numbers.

### Structure C
- Para 1 (20 words): introduce the issue and why it shouldn't be ignored. Straightforward, informative.
- Para 2 (30 words): a direct answer immediately. Bold the location name. Explain the process and how the business solves the problem; do not name the business here. Vary where the bolded location appears across posts, not always the same paragraph position.
- Para 3 (20 words): start with an emoji plus "Get in touch," "Schedule," or "Call." Name the business once here. Focus on what the customer will experience: smooth service, safety, clear communication. No phone numbers.

### Cost-related regular topics
When a regular topic asks about cost, research and state a real range for that service and location, then the factors that affect it. Never invent a number; use the client's pricing reference when one exists.

## Post Type 2: Offer Post

AIDA format, for promoting a specific offer (free quote, free inspection, seasonal discount).
- Para 1 (30 words): the problem the customer faces from not knowing the answer (cost, risk, etc.)
- Para 2 (50 words): introduce the business, bold its name, state the offer, what it includes, how it benefits the customer
- Para 3 (20 words): CTA starting with a phone emoji, call or contact to claim the offer

## Post Type 3: Review Post

Exception to the shared word count: 35 to 40 words maximum, no title.

Input is a review screenshot or link. Extract the business name, customer name, service performed, and specific praise mentioned.

Rotate the opening across a business's review posts, don't always lead the same way:
- Business-first: lead with the business name and what it did
- Client-first: lead with the customer's name and what they said
- Quote-first: lead with the specific thing they praised, before naming anyone

Never use the exact phrase "Just ask [name]." Mention the business name once. Never name employees. No question sentences. No corporate jargon ("highly efficient," "customer-centric"). No em dashes, no comma-plus-ing verbs. Use line breaks for mobile readability.

Rotate the CTA line too, vary the emoji and verb across Schedule, Call, Contact, and don't repeat the same CTA as the business's last 2 to 3 review posts.

## Post Type 4: Entity Post

For "tree service near [Landmark]" posts. The target is the homes and businesses around the landmark, not the landmark itself.

- Para 1 (20 words): a direct statement that the business offers care for residents around the landmark. Name the business.
- Para 2 (40 words): freely cover, in any order, service names, local tree species, local area features, problems solved, and services offered, using the landmark research given
- Para 3 (25 words, one sentence): start with "Call" plus a phone emoji. Reassure on reliability, fast
