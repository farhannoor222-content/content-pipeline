## Details about Specific Trees in the Area

Greensboro properties feature a mix of native and ornamental tree species, each with unique maintenance needs and risk profiles. Understanding common species, regional weather patterns, and seasonal challenges helps homeowners anticipate care needs and recognize when professional intervention is necessary. This knowledge also allows the service to tailor recommendations based on tree type, age, and environmental exposure throughout the year.

## Common Species

* Sweetgum, Tulip poplar, and American beech are hallmark native species in the city
* Red Maple is among the most common trees regionally
* Loblolly Pine frequently found in larger residential lots
* Southern Magnolia popular for its evergreen, ornamental appeal
* Crepe Myrtle widely planted for seasonal color
* Dogwood common in smaller residential yards
* Live Oak valued for shade and longevity
* Bradford Pear common but prone to structural failure
* Eastern White Pine present in wooded properties
* Tree-of-Heaven and Princess Tree are invasive concerns

## Weather Conditions for Trees

* Hot, humid summers stress trees and increase pest activity
* Cold winter snaps can cause limb and bark damage
* Heavy spring storms bring high winds and lightning
* Seasonal thunderstorms increase risk of fallen limbs
* Ice storms add weight, causing branch breakage
* Drought periods weaken root systems and tree health
* Heavy rainfall can lead to soil saturation and uprooting
* Hurricane remnants occasionally bring severe wind damage
* Humidity promotes fungal disease and decay in trees
* Sudden temperature swings stress tree health overall

## Seasonal Challenges

* Spring: storm damage and rapid new growth management
* Summer: pest infestations and heat-related stress
* Fall: leaf litter buildup and pre-winter pruning needs
* Winter: ice damage and dormant-season trimming opportunities
* Year-round: managing invasive or weak-wooded species like Bradford Pear
* Storm season increases demand for emergency response
* Seasonal color changes prompt aesthetic trimming requests
* Pollen season raises concerns about tree health visibility
* Post-storm cleanup spikes after major weather events
* Pre-storm prep trims reduce risk before hurricane season
