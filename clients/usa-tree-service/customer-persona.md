## Details about Specific Trees in the Area

### Native Species

* Cottonwood: Fast-growing with shallow roots; prone to branch failure in storms; produces fluffy seeds that create cleanup issues.
   * Birch: Attractive white bark; susceptible to bronze birch borer; requires regular watering during drought.
   * Willow: Thrives near water sources; extensive root systems that seek water pipes; brittle branches break easily in wind.
   * Chokecherry: Small native tree/shrub; produces berries for wildlife; susceptible to black knot fungus.

### Common Evergreens

* Mountain Hemlock: Conical shape with drooping leader; grows slowly; prefers partial shade.
   * Western Hemlock: Tall with feathery foliage; shade-tolerant; vulnerable to drought and root rot.
   * Pacific Silver Fir: Symmetrical with silvery needles; needs consistent moisture; slow-growing.
   * Western Redcedar: Long-lived with scale-like foliage; drought-sensitive; vulnerable to cedar bark beetles.
   * Yellow Cedar: Less common; distinctive yellow-green foliage; sensitive to climate change.

### Common Tree Problems in Walla Walla

* Drought stress during hot, dry summers
   * Fire blight affecting fruit trees
   * Pine beetle infestations in conifers
   * Winter storm damage from ice loading
   * Root diseases in aging trees
   * Lightning strikes during summer storms
   * Girdling roots causing slow decline

## Weather Conditions for Trees

### Seasonal Challenges

* Winter: Ice storms causing limb breakage; heavy snow load on evergreens; freezing damage to less hardy species.
   * Spring: Late frost affecting new growth; heavy rains causing root saturation; wind storms during seasonal transitions.
   * Summer: Drought stress requiring supplemental water; heat damage to exposed trees; increased insect activity.
   * Fall: Preparation for winter conditions; leaf cleanup needs; final pruning before dormancy.
