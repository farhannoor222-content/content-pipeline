# JM Ezzy Tree Service Customer Profile

## Target Client Overview

Davis Tree Service’s core customers are property owners and managers in Ponca City and nearby north-central Oklahoma communities who need safe, dependable tree care. They value insured crews, clear estimates, responsive communication, thorough cleanup, and practical solutions for hazardous, overgrown, storm-damaged, or inconvenient trees.

## Demographics

* Age: Primarily 35–70; includes younger homeowners, established families, retirees, landlords, and commercial property managers.
* Income: Middle to upper-middle income; enough household or business budget to pay for professional, insured tree work rather than risky DIY removal.
* Education: Mixed, but many are practical decision-makers with trade, professional, management, agricultural, or small-business backgrounds.
* Home Value: Moderate to higher-value owner-occupied homes, rental properties, and commercial sites where tree damage or poor access creates real financial risk.
* Location: Ponca City, Blackwell, Newkirk, and nearby north-central Oklahoma; also Arkansas City, Wellington, Udall, and Winfield, Kansas.

## Property Type

* Historic homes: Older, established neighborhoods with mature trees, tighter lots, and greater concern about limbs near roofs, driveways, fences, and utilities.
* Modern single-family homes: Suburban and rural residences needing routine pruning, removals, stump grinding, storm cleanup, and safer yard access.
* High-end townhouses: Less common, but relevant where shared grounds, limited access, appearance, and liability make professional service important.
* Small multi-family properties: Rentals and small apartment properties where owners prioritize tenant safety, fast response, cleanup, and predictable scheduling.

## Key Characteristics of the Customer

* Responsible for a property where trees affect safety, access, appearance, insurance exposure, or daily use.
* Often calls only when a problem becomes visible: dead limbs, leaning trees, storm damage, blocked driveways, or roots and debris.
* Wants competence and reassurance: an explanation of the problem, safe completion, protection of nearby structures, and a clean site.
* Values local ownership, direct communication, honest recommendations, and a company that actually shows up as scheduled.
* May be a homeowner, landlord, business owner, farm/ranch owner, or manager responsible for multiple properties.

## Decision-Making Process

## Initial Trigger Events

* A storm leaves hanging, broken, or fallen limbs.
* A tree looks dead, diseased, leaning, or dangerously close to a roof, vehicle, fence, power line, or walkway.
* Overgrowth blocks light, views, driveways, sidewalks, equipment access, or property lines.
* A home sale, renovation, insurance concern, tenant complaint, or scheduled maintenance creates urgency.

## Research Phase

* Customers search online for local tree removal, trimming, emergency service, stump grinding, or land clearing.
* They compare reviews, photos, response time, insurance, licensing, service area, and whether the company offers free estimates.
* Many ask neighbors, family, coworkers, or local Facebook/community groups for recommendations.

## Evaluation Criteria

* Safety record, insurance, and ability to handle difficult or hazardous removals.
* Clear scope of work, transparent pricing, and no-pressure estimates.
* Fast communication, dependable scheduling, and emergency availability.
* Property protection, debris cleanup, and respect for landscaping, fences, roofs, and access routes.

## Quote Process

* Customers typically request a free, no-obligation estimate by phone, website form, or referral.
* They want a straightforward explanation of what will be removed, trimmed, ground, hauled, or left on-site.
* Price matters, but vague quotes, missed appointments, and unclear cleanup expectations are bigger objections than a fair professional price.

## Environmental Values

* Many customers want unhealthy or hazardous trees removed responsibly, but prefer preservation, pruning, or selective removal when safe and practical.
* They respond to responsible debris handling, avoiding unnecessary removal, and protecting the surrounding yard and landscape.

## Personal Values

* Safety for family, tenants, employees, customers, and visitors.
* Protecting their biggest investment: the home, rental, business property, or land.
* Reliability, honesty, local accountability, and work done without drama or property damage.

## Details about Specific Trees in the Area

Ponca City’s tree needs are shaped by mature residential canopies, seasonal storms, drought stress, and trees planted close to homes, driveways, fences, and utilities. Customers often need help identifying whether a tree can be preserved through pruning or should be removed because of structural risk.

## Common Species

* Oak, maple, elm, hackberry, cottonwood, pecan, and other mature shade trees common in established neighborhoods.
* Ornamental and smaller trees such as redbud, crabapple, Bradford/Callery pear, crape myrtle, and fruit trees.
* Evergreens and windbreak species around rural properties, farms, and open lots.
* Older, oversized, or storm-damaged trees near roofs, driveways, fences, and power lines often require professional assessment.

## Weather Conditions for Trees

* Hot, dry summers can create drought stress, weakened growth, and greater vulnerability to pests and disease.
* High winds, thunderstorms, ice, and winter weather can break limbs, split trunks, and create sudden hazards.
* Wet periods followed by dry spells can stress root systems and contribute to decline in vulnerable trees.
* Storm-damaged or hanging limbs should be treated as hazardous, especially near structures or electrical lines.

## Seasonal Challenges

* Spring: New growth, storm exposure, pruning needs, and early pest or disease concerns.
* Summer: Drought stress, heavy canopy growth, storm damage, and access issues around active outdoor spaces.
* Fall: Leaf drop reveals branch structure but adds debris to roofs, gutters, driveways, and drainage areas.
* Winter: Structural defects are easier to see in bare deciduous trees, but ice, snow, wind, and frozen ground can complicate emergency work.

## Customer Journey

The customer journey usually begins when a tree problem feels unsafe, disruptive, or too difficult to handle personally. It then moves through local research, estimate comparison, scheduling, safe service, and follow-up—often faster after storms or when utilities, public access, or property damage are involved.

1. Awareness Phase

### Initial Triggers

* A homeowner notices dead limbs, splits, leaning trunks, storm damage, or overgrowth.
* A tree blocks a driveway, sidewalk, view, equipment route, or building access.
* A tenant, neighbor, insurance agent, realtor, or contractor raises a safety or liability concern.
* Severe weather creates fallen limbs, hanging branches, or urgent access problems.

### Information Gathering

* Customers search Google for local tree removal, trimming, stump grinding, or emergency tree service.
* They check reviews, photos, service descriptions, response time, insurance, licensing, and service area.
* They ask neighbors, family, coworkers, and local community groups for recommendations.
2. Consideration Phase

### Research Activities

* Compare two or more local providers on scope, credentials, equipment, cleanup, and communication.
* Look for proof of insured service, emergency availability, crane capability, and experience with similar properties.
* Review estimates for clarity: what will be removed, trimmed, ground, hauled, or left on-site.
* Consider whether the company can explain risks and options rather than simply quoting a price.
3. Decision Phase

### Final Considerations

* Choose the provider that offers the best combination of safety, trust, responsiveness, scope clarity, and fair value.
* Favor a company that provides a written estimate, explains cleanup, and does not pressure the customer.
* Prioritize insured, capable crews for hazardous, large, or hard-to-reach trees.
* Confirm scheduling, payment terms, access needs, and whether utility, permit, or boundary issues may apply.
4. Service Phase

### Pre-service

* Confirm the approved scope, arrival window, parking, gate/access needs, pets, and cleanup expectations.
* Identify nearby structures, landscaping, fences, vehicles, septic areas, and utility concerns before work begins.
* Prepare the site and establish a safe work zone.
* Communicate clearly if weather, access, or unforeseen conditions require a change to the plan.
5. Follow-up
* Conduct a final walkthrough to confirm completed work, property protection, and cleanup.
* Provide documentation of the work performed, remaining recommendations, and future maintenance priorities—especially useful for landlords and commercial managers.
* Offer seasonal reminders, preventive-maintenance scheduling, and answers to post-service questions.
* Request a review and encourage referrals when the customer feels the company communicated honestly, worked safely, and left the property clean.
