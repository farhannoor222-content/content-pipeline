## Overview of the Typical Client

### The typical Proza Tree Service client falls into several distinct categories

* Property tenure: Owns their home for 8+ years, now dealing with maturing trees planted during initial landscaping or inherited from previous owners

* Decision timing: Makes non-emergency decisions within 1-4 weeks of first noticing an issue
* Budget consciousness: Willing to pay for quality but researches options and expects detailed estimates
* Home pride level: High - views trees as important components of property value and curb appeal

## Decision-Making Process (Detailed Stages)

1. Initial Awareness (Day 1)
   * Notice visual problem (dead branches, leaning tree)
   * Experience triggering event (storm damage, fallen limb)
   * Receive comment from visitor about tree condition
   * Discover maintenance issue during other yard work
   * Average time in this stage: 1-7 days (non-emergency)
2. Information Gathering (Days 2-10)
   * Google searches: "tree removal near me," "tree service North Tonawanda"
   * Ask 2-3 neighbors for recommendations
   * Check neighborhood Facebook groups for mentions
   * Read Google reviews for local services (average 6-10 reviews read)
   * Look at company websites for service descriptions
   * Average time in this stage: 3-7 days
3. Initial Contact (Days 7-14)
   * Call or email 2-3 companies for consultations
   * Schedule on-site visits (typically within 3-5 days)
   * Prepare questions about process, timeline, cleanup
   * Consider what specific tree issues to point out
   * Average time in this stage: 2-5 days
4. Evaluation (Days 12-21)
   * Meet with representatives on property
   * Discuss options (removal, trimming, treatment)
   * Receive and compare quotes (price range expectations)
   * Check credentials (insurance, certifications)
   * Assess professionalism of consultation
   * Compare recommendations between companies
   * Average time in this stage: 5-10 days
5. Decision Factors (Days 14-21)
   * Primary factors:
   * Price (40%)
   * Professionalism/trust (25%)
   * Availability/timing (15%)
   * Recommendation strength (10%)
   * Scope of services offered (10%)
   * Secondary considerations:
   * Clean-up promises
   * Equipment used
   * Crew size
   * Payment terms
   * Warranty offered
   * Average time in this stage: 2-7 days
6. Final Selection (Days 14-28)
   * Household discussion about options
   * Possible negotiation on price or scope
   * Schedule service date
   * Make any required deposits
   * Average time to decision from initial awareness: 2-4 weeks for non-emergency work
7. Emergency Timeline Compression
   * All stages compressed into 24-48 hours
   * Fewer companies contacted (often just 1-2)
   * Price sensitivity reduced by urgency
   * Insurance considerations more prominent
   * Safety concerns outweigh other factors

## Types of Trees in Western New York (Detailed Profiles)

* Maple Varieties
   * Sugar Maple (Acer saccharum)
   * Height: 60-75 feet at maturity
   * Lifespan: 300+ years when healthy
   * Common issues: Verticillium wilt, leaf scorch, winter salt damage
   * Maintenance needs: Periodic thinning, deadwood removal
   * Value: Significant contribution to property value, fall color
   * Notable: New York State tree, brilliant fall colors
   * Red Maple (Acer rubrum)
   * Height: 40-60 feet at maturity
   * Lifespan: 80-150 years
   * Common issues: Verticillium wilt, chlorosis in alkaline soils
   * Maintenance needs: Corrective pruning when young
   * Client concerns: Surface roots affecting lawns and walkways
   * Notable: Earlier fall color changes than sugar maple
   * Silver Maple (Acer saccharinum)
   * Height: 50-80 feet at maturity
   * Lifespan: 100-150 years
   * Common issues: Weak wood structure, storm damage susceptibility
   * Maintenance needs: Regular structural pruning to reduce failure risk
   * Client concerns: Aggressive roots threatening foundations and pipes
   * Notable: Fast-growing, common in older neighborhoods
* Oak Species
   * Red Oak (Quercus rubra)
   * Height: 60-75 feet at maturity
   * Lifespan: 200-300 years
   * Common issues: Oak wilt concerns, gypsy moth susceptibility
   * Maintenance needs: Minimal once established, avoid summer pruning
   * Value: High contribution to property values
   * Notable: Strong branching structure, excellent shade
   * White Oak (Quercus alba)
   * Height: 50-80 feet at maturity
   * Lifespan: 300+ years
   * Common issues: Anthracnose, two-lined chestnut borer
   * Maintenance needs: Minimal pruning required, avoid root disturbance
   * Client concerns: Acorn production, slower growth rate
   * Notable: Highest quality among oak species
* Ash Trees (Fraxinus sp.)
   * Height: 40-60 feet at maturity
   * Lifespan: Naturally 150-300 years, now threatened
   * Critical issue: Emerald Ash Borer infestation throughout region
   * Current status: Majority require removal or intensive treatment
   * Client concerns: Rapid decline, falling branches when infected
   * Treatment options: Expensive multi-year treatments or removal
   * Post-removal: Stump grinding and replacement planting
   * Regional note: Significant percentage of urban canopy being lost
* Evergreens
   * Eastern White Pine (Pinus strobus)
   * Height: 50-80 feet in landscape settings
   * Common issues: White pine blister rust, weevils, winter burn
   * Maintenance needs: Avoid lower limb removal
   * Client concerns: Needle drop, sap production
   * Blue Spruce (Picea pungens)
   * Height: 30-60 feet in landscape settings
   * Common issues: Needle cast diseases, spider mites
   * Maintenance needs: Monitoring for discoloration
   * Client concerns: Browning needles, dieback in older specimens
   * Norway Spruce (Picea abies)
   * Height: 40-60 feet in landscape settings
   * Common issues: Less disease prone than blue spruce
   * Maintenance needs: Minimal pruning required
   * Client concerns: Lower branch dieback in mature specimens
* Ornamental Species
   * Flowering Cherry (Prunus sp.)
   * Height: 15-30 feet depending on variety
   * Common issues: Trunk cankers, borer insects, leaf spot
   * Maintenance needs: Regular monitoring for disease
   * Client concerns: Short lifespan (20-30 years), sudden decline
   * Flowering Dogwood (Cornus florida)
   * Height: 15-25 feet at maturity
   * Common issues: Anthracnose, powdery mildew, borers
   * Maintenance needs: Careful pruning, mulching
   * Client concerns: Difficult growing conditions in WNY climate
   * Crabapple (Malus sp.)
   * Height: 15-25 feet depending on variety
   * Common issues: Apple scab, fireblight, Japanese beetles
   * Maintenance needs: Structural pruning when young
   * Client concerns: Fruit drop, disease susceptibility
* Other Native Trees
   * American Beech (Fagus grandifolia)
   * Height: 50-70 feet at maturity
   * Common issues: Beech bark disease prevalent in region
   * Maintenance needs: Careful pruning, avoid wounds
   * Client concerns: Declining health throughout region
   * Birch Species (Betula sp.)
   * Height: 30-50 feet depending on species
   * Common issues: Bronze birch borer, leaf miners
   * Maintenance needs: Regular monitoring for borer signs
   * Client concerns: Relatively short lifespan in landscape settings
   * Black Walnut (Juglans nigra)
   * Height: 50-75 feet at maturity
   * Common issues: Allelopathic effects on other plants
   * Maintenance needs: Fruit cleanup, strategic location
   * Client concerns: Staining from nuts, landscape limitations
* Near-Water Species
   * Weeping Willow (Salix babylonica)
   * Height: 30-50 feet at maturity
   * Common issues: Weak wood, aggressive roots, short lifespan
   * Maintenance needs: Regular pruning to remove weak branches
   * Client concerns: Drain/sewer line invasions, messy debris
   * Eastern Cottonwood (Populus deltoides)
   * Height: 50-80 feet at maturity
   * Common issues: Weak wood, cotton production, aggressive roots
   * Maintenance needs: Regular hazard assessment
   * Client concerns: Cotton mess in June, frequent storm damage

   * Winter Damage Factors:
   * Lake effect snow accumulation causing branch breakage

   * Road salt spray affecting roadside trees
   * Freeze-thaw cycles creating bark splits
   * Winter desiccation in evergreens
   * Soil Condition Factors:
   * Clay-heavy soils in many suburban developments
   * Compacted soils from construction in newer neighborhoods
   * Variable pH levels across the region
   * Urban soil contamination concerns
   * Poor drainage in many flat areas
   * Climate Considerations:
   * USDA Hardiness Zone 5b-6a
   * Lake effect weather patterns
   * High humidity contributing to fungal issues
   * Strong prevailing winds near lake areas
   * Urban heat island effects in Buffalo metro
   * Regional Pest Issues:
   * Emerald Ash Borer (critical infestation)
