## Personal Values

* Family first: Protects home environment for children, grandchildren

Home represents the central gathering place for family life, Sunday dinners, holiday celebrations, and creating childhood memories. Parents prioritize non-toxic, low-VOC paints for children's bedrooms and play areas. They schedule projects around kids' school calendars, sports seasons, and nap times. Grandparents prepare homes for visiting grandchildren with fresh, welcoming spaces. Family safety, comfort, and pride drive investment decisions more than personal aesthetic preferences alone.

* Community pride: Wants home to enhance neighborhood appeal

Well-maintained homes reflect personal values and respect for neighbors who maintain their properties too. Homeowners feel social pressure when their peeling paint becomes the neighborhood eyesore. They notice when multiple neighbors paint and feel motivated to keep up appearances. Church members, coworkers, and friends drive by regularly—the home represents their reputation publicly. Contributing to attractive, cohesive neighborhoods increases everyone's property values and community desirability.

* Financial responsibility: Budgets carefully, saves for projects

These aren't impulsive spenders—painting projects are planned 6-12 months in advance with dedicated savings. They create home maintenance budgets annually, prioritizing projects by necessity and ROI. Emergency funds are protected; painting only happens when savings are adequate. They comparison shop, negotiate respectfully, and expect value. Credit cards are paid monthly; financing is avoided unless absolutely necessary. Every dollar spent on painting competes with college funds, retirement savings, and family vacations.

* Quality over flash: Prefers solid workmanship to trendy extras

Durable, long-lasting results matter more than Instagram-worthy accent walls or trendy color choices. They choose proven Sherwin-Williams or Benjamin Moore over boutique brands. Proper surface preparation, quality primer, and two-coat coverage aren't negotiable shortcuts. They'd rather delay projects than accept rushed, substandard work. Neutral, timeless colors win over bold statements that might need repainting sooner. Workmanship warranty and contractor reputation trump lowest price consistently.

* Southern hospitality: Expects respectful, courteous service

Contractors should remove shoes at the door, say "yes ma'am" and "yes sir," and treat homes like their grandmother's house. Friendliness without inappropriate familiarity—professional warmth, not buddy-buddy casualness. They notice whether crews clean up daily, protect landscaping, and avoid foul language around children. Thank-you notes, follow-up calls, and genuine care about satisfaction matter enormously. Rudeness, dismissiveness, or condescension ends business relationships immediately regardless of quality.

* Self-reliance mixed with pragmatism: Knows when to call professionals

Kentucky culture values DIY capability and hands-on skills traditionally. These homeowners have tried painting themselves and know the physical demands, time commitments, and skill limitations firsthand. Ladders feel less safe at 50 than 30. Weekend time is precious now—they'd rather watch kids' games than scrape paint. They recognize professional efficiency: a crew completes in three days what would take them three weekends. Calling contractors isn't admitting defeat—it's smart resource allocation.

## Details about Specific Problems in the Area

### Common Building Types

* 1990s-2000s subdivisions: Vinyl siding, wood trim needing refresh
* 1970s-1980s ranches: Dated interiors, original bathrooms
* Historic homes (pre-1950): Lead paint, plaster walls, wood siding
* Modern construction (2010+): Minimal maintenance needs currently

### Weather Conditions

* Humid subtropical climate (Köppen Cfa classification)
* Hot, humid summers: 90-95°F, high moisture = mold risk
* Cold winters: 23-39°F averages, occasional below 0°F
* Spring: Wettest season: Heavy rains, thunderstorms, painting delays
* Fall: Ideal painting season: Low humidity, mild temps (50-83°F)
* Record low: -37°F (1994) - extreme cold stress on exteriors
* Annual precipitation: ~50 inches/year

### Seasonal Challenges

* Spring (March-May): Too wet for exterior work, interior focus
* Summer (June-August): Heat/humidity challenges, mold growth
* Fall (September-November): PRIME painting season, high demand
* Winter (December-February): Interior projects, limited exterior work

### Climate-specific Issues

* Mold/mildew on siding, bathrooms (humidity)
* Paint fading/chalking from UV exposure
* Wood rot on trim, doors, siding (moisture)
* Peeling/blistering from humidity trapped under paint
* Caulk failure from temperature extremes
* Ice damage on gutters, trim (winter)

## Customer Journey

1. Awareness Phase

### Initial Triggers

* Notices peeling paint during yard work
* Neighbor completes beautiful painting project
* Spring cleaning reveals dingy, dated interiors
* Real estate agent suggests painting before listing
* Water stain appears on ceiling/wall
* New home purchase with outdated colors

### Information Gathering

* Google: "best painters Shelbyville KY"
* Asks on Nextdoor: "Who has recommendations for painters?"
* Checks Facebook for local business pages
* Drives around neighborhood looking for yard signs
* Asks coworker who recently painted
2. Consideration Phase

### Research Activities

* Reads Google reviews (looks for 4.5+ stars, detailed reviews)
* Visits company websites (looking for local feel, photos)
* Checks Facebook for recent projects, customer interactions
* Calls 3-5 contractors for basic info, responsiveness test
* Requests estimates from top 3-4 choices
* Compares proposals, pricing, professionalism
* Checks insurance, licensing, Better Business Bureau

### Evaluation Process (1-3 weeks)

* Reviews written estimates for clarity, completeness
* Discusses options with spouse/partner
* Considers timeline fit with family schedule
* Weighs price vs. perceived value
* Revisits online reviews of top choices
* May request references, call previous customers
* Gut check: "Do I trust these people in my home?"
3. Decision Phase

### Final Considerations

* Price comparison (rarely chooses cheapest)
* Timeline availability
* Communication quality throughout estimate process
* Professionalism of presentation
* Comfort level with crew/company
* Warranty and guarantee terms
* Payment terms flexibility

### Decision Made

* Calls to confirm, asks final questions
* Signs contract, schedules deposit
* Receives pre-project checklist
* Begins preparation (moving furniture, etc.)
* Tells neighbors about upcoming project
4. Service Phase

### Pre-service

* Receives confirmation call 2 days before
* Final prep (clears work areas, pets secured)
* Meets crew on day 1, walkthrough
* Daily check-ins via text/photos

### During Service

* Monitors progress
* Addresses questions as they arise
* Appreciates daily cleanup
* Watches transformation unfold
* May share progress photos on social media

### Project Completion

* Final walkthrough with crew leader
* Touch-up list created, addressed
* Makes final payment
* Receives care instructions, warranty info
5. Follow-up Phase

### Immediate (1-7 days)

* Enjoys newly painted space
* Shows off to family, friends
* Posts before/after photos on social media
* Receives follow-up call from company

### 30 Days

* Check-in call: "How's everything holding up?"
* Any touch-ups needed addressed quickly
* Review request (if satisfied)

### Long-term (6 months-2 years)

* Refers friends, family, neighbors
* Calls back for additional projects
* Responds to annual check-in
* Maintains relationship with trusted contractor
