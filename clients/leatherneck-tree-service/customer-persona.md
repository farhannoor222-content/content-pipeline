## **2. DECISION-MAKING PROCESS**

Understanding how customers decide to hire a tree service reveals where Leatherneck can influence the journey most effectively:

### **Stage 1 – Trigger Event**

A specific event prompts the customer to take action. This could be a storm-damaged limb, a dead tree that has become an eyesore, a neighbor's comment, a home inspection report flagging a hazardous tree, or the visible spread of disease.

### **Stage 2 – Awareness ****&**** Research**

The customer conducts a Google search ('tree removal near me,' 'tree trimming Beaver County PA'), checks Facebook groups or Nextdoor, or asks a neighbor for a recommendation. They scan 3–5 options and begin comparing. Google reviews, website quality, and photos heavily influence shortlisting.

### **Stage 3 – Shortlisting**

The customer narrows to 2–3 companies based on: proximity to their area, years in business, reviews and ratings, veteran-owned status (for those who value it), and whether a free estimate is offered. Leatherneck's 10+ years of experience and veteran-owned identity provide strong differentiation at this stage.

### **Stage 4 – Estimate Request**

The customer contacts companies for a free estimate—by phone call, website form, or Facebook message. Speed of response is critical here; a prompt call-back or same-day estimate visit can win the job before competitors respond.

### **Stage 5 – Evaluation**

The customer compares estimates based on: total price, scope of work described, professionalism of the estimator, cleanup included, timeline, and payment terms. Leatherneck's competitive pricing and veteran-owned trust factor are key differentiators.

### **Stage 6 – Decision ****&**** Booking**

The final decision is usually made within 1–7 days for non-emergency work, and same-day to next-day for emergency situations. Approval from a spouse or co-owner is common.

### **Stage 7 – Post-Service Evaluation**

After the job, the customer assesses cleanup quality, professionalism, whether work matched the estimate, and overall satisfaction. Positive outcomes drive referrals and repeat bookings.

## **3. PROBLEMS ****&**** PAIN POINTS**

## **Primary Pain Points**

* Storm Damage & Emergency Situations: A fallen tree or large limb blocking a driveway, roof, or vehicle causes immediate stress, financial anxiety, and safety risk. Customers need fast response with a clear resolution path.

* Fear of Property Damage: Dead, diseased, or structurally compromised trees create constant worry—especially homeowners whose trees overhang roofs, garages, fences, or power lines.

* Visible Tree Decline Without Knowing Why: Customers notice their tree is losing leaves early, has unusual bark, or looks 'sick,' but lack the knowledge to diagnose or respond correctly.

* Overgrown or Unmanaged Trees: Trees that have grown too large, into power lines, or over neighboring properties create liability concerns and conflict with neighbors or utility companies.

* Not Knowing Who to Trust: The tree service industry has a significant presence of uninsured, unlicensed, or inexperienced operators. Customers fear poor workmanship or being overcharged.

* Unclear Pricing: Customers hate calling for estimates and receiving vague pricing, overly high quotes, or unexpected add-ons at the end of the job.

* Mess and Cleanup: Many customers dread the aftermath—wood debris, leaves, and sawdust left behind. They want a complete, professional cleanup included in the service.

* Land Clearing Complexity: Property owners who need land cleared for construction, fencing, or landscaping don't know where to start and need a single vendor who can handle the entire scope.

* Firewood Needs: Homeowners with wood-burning fireplaces or stoves need a reliable source of seasoned firewood, ideally from trees already being removed on their property.

* Pest and Disease Uncertainty: With regional threats like Emerald Ash Borer and Beech Leaf Disease, customers need expert guidance on whether a tree can be saved or must be removed.

## **Secondary Pain Points**

* Difficulty Finding Availability: During peak seasons (spring, post-storm), tree service companies are booked out—customers fear they can't get help when needed most.

* Stump Eyesores: After tree removal, leftover stumps are unsightly, a mowing hazard, attract insects, and prevent new planting.

* HOA & Municipal Requirements: Some customers face pressure from HOAs or local codes to maintain or remove specific trees within a compliance window.

* Insurance Claims Support: Storm damage clients often need documentation for homeowner insurance claims and prefer a contractor familiar with the process.
