# Show Me Tree Service Customer Profile

* Customer Profile: Show Me Tree Service

## Overview

The typical Show Me Tree Service client is a Jacksonville homeowner aged 42–58 with a household income of $65,000–$150,000, living in an established neighborhood with mature trees. They've hit a trigger point — storm damage, a hazardous tree, or an upcoming sale — and want a licensed, insured local company offering a free written estimate, fair pricing, and dependable cleanup.

## Demographics

* Age: 35–65, with a concentration between 42–58. Homeowners old enough to have settled into a long-term property and built equity, young enough to still be doing active yard projects.
* Income: 150,000+ household income. Many are dual-income households or established single professionals.
* Education: Majority hold a bachelor's degree or higher; a meaningful share work in management, healthcare, law, or business ownership.
* Home Value: 650,000, with pockets above $650,000 in San Marco, Ortega, and Deerwood Center.
* Location: Concentrated in established Jacksonville neighborhoods with mature tree canopy — San Marco, Ortega, Mandarin, Lake Lucina, Baymeadows, and Southside Estates, with additional demand from Downtown Jacksonville condo-adjacent single-family pockets.
* Property Type:
   * Historic homes: 20%
   * Modern single-family homes: 55%
   * High-end townhouses: 10%
   * Small multi-family properties: 15%

Key characteristics of the Customer: Owns a property with established, often large, trees planted decades ago. Values curb appeal and takes pride in how the property looks from the street. Has a working knowledge of home maintenance but defers to professionals for anything involving height, chainsaws, or heavy equipment. Tends to be risk-averse about liability and safety, especially around storm season. Wants a company that shows up when they say they will and gives a straight answer on price.

## Decision-Making Process

1. Initial Trigger Events:
   * A storm (hurricane, tropical system, or strong thunderstorm) has damaged or dropped a tree or limb
   * A tree is visibly leaning, cracked, hollow, or dropping large limbs
   * Roots are lifting a driveway, sidewalk, or foundation
   * Preparing a property for sale and needing the yard to look its best
   * A neighbor's tree service just finished a job nearby and it caught their eye
   * Insurance company or HOA flagged a tree as a liability
2. Research Phase:
   * Google search for "tree removal near me" or "tree service Jacksonville FL"
   * Checking Google reviews and star ratings first
   * Asking neighbors or a local Facebook/Nextdoor group for recommendations
   * Looking for how long the company has been in business
   * Checking whether the company is licensed and insured before calling
3. Evaluation Criteria:
   * Proof of insurance and licensing (protects against liability if a worker is injured or property is damaged)
   * Whether an on-site estimate is free and requires no deposit
   * Response time, particularly for storm-damage situations
   * Clear, written pricing with no surprise add-ons
   * Reviews mentioning cleanup quality and punctuality
4. Quote Process: Customer typically calls or fills out a web form, describes the tree issue, and requests an on-site visit. They compare 2–3 quotes before committing, weighing price against how confident they felt in the person who came out to look at the tree.

## Environmental Values

Prefers to keep healthy trees standing whenever possible rather than defaulting to removal. Interested in native plant selection for any replanting, since native species tend to need less water and upkeep. Cares about the shade and property value that mature trees provide and doesn't want to lose a tree unnecessarily.

## Personal Values

Pride of ownership. A well-kept yard reflects well on the household. Wants to be a responsible neighbor — a falling limb or overgrown tree affecting someone else's property is a real worry. Appreciates honesty over an upsell.

## Psychographics

Lifestyle: Busy working household with limited weekend time for yard work beyond mowing and light upkeep. Outsources anything requiring specialized equipment or working at height. Likely already pays for lawn care or pest control and is comfortable hiring outside help for the home.

Shopping Habits: Reads reviews closely before calling. Prefers a company that gives a written estimate over a verbal number. Willing to pay more for a company with clean, insured, professional workers over the cheapest bid.

Long-term Worries: A tree falling on the house, car, or a person during a storm. Rising insurance premiums tied to storm-related claims. Root damage to plumbing, driveways, or foundations. Losing established shade and privacy if a large tree has to come down.

Service-related Concerns: Whether debris and stumps will actually be cleaned up afterward. Whether the crew will damage the lawn, fence, or nearby plants. Whether the company will show up on the day promised, especially during peak storm season.

## Influencers/Decision Supporters

* Primary Influencers: Spouse or partner, since tree removal decisions are usually a joint household call involving cost and property appearance.
* Secondary Influencers: Neighbors who've had similar work done, family members with home maintenance experience.
* Professional Resources: Real estate agents (ahead of a home sale), insurance adjusters, HOA representatives, arborists.
* Online Resources: Google reviews, Nextdoor, local Facebook community groups, the company website and photo galleries.

Financial Concerns: Whether the price is fair for the size and difficulty of the job. Whether payment plans or financing are available for larger removal jobs. Wanting a written estimate before any work begins.

* Service Delivery: Wants a defined start and finish time, a clean site afterward, and clear communication if weather delays the job.

Technical Concerns: Whether the crew has the right equipment for large or hard-to-reach trees. Whether stump grinding will be thorough enough to allow replanting or sod.

Regulatory Concerns: Whether a permit is needed to remove a tree, especially in historic districts or HOA-governed neighborhoods. Whether the company handles any required permitting.

## Key Motivations

* Safety Motivations: Removing a hazard before it causes injury or property damage. Peace of mind ahead of hurricane season.
* Aesthetic Motivations: A trimmed, healthy tree canopy that improves how the whole property looks. Removing dead or unsightly trees that drag down curb appeal.

Practical Motivations: Preventing root damage to hardscaping and utility lines. Clearing space for a new structure, pool, or landscaping project. Improving sunlight to the lawn or garden.

* Environmental Motivations: Keeping mature, healthy trees whenever possible. Choosing native replacement plantings that support the local ecosystem.

## What They Want to Achieve

* Property Goals: A yard that looks well-maintained year-round and holds or improves home value.
* Personal Goals: Not having to think or worry about a risky tree every time a storm rolls through.
* Long-term Objectives: A yard that requires less ongoing intervention — healthy trees that won't need emergency attention later.

## Details about Specific Trees in the Area

Common Species: Southern live oak, laurel oak, water oak, longleaf and slash pine, southern magnolia, sweetgum, sabal palm, and crepe myrtle are common across Jacksonville yards.

Weather Conditions for Trees: Jacksonville's humid subtropical climate, combined with hurricane season (June–November), puts regular wind and saturated-soil stress on root systems. Heavy summer rain and occasional drought stretches also affect tree health.

Seasonal Challenges: Hurricane season brings the highest risk of limb failure and uprooting, particularly for water oaks and laurel oaks, which are prone to interior decay. Spring pollen and growth spurts can reveal weak limbs. Winter is typically the best window for major trimming, since trees are dormant and less stressed by cuts.
