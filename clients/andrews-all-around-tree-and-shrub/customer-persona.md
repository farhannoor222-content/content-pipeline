## Key Characteristics of the Customer

## Decision-Making Process

## Initial Trigger Events

## Storm Damage Requiring Emergency Tree Service

Severe weather events create immediate need for professional intervention. Clients experiencing storm damage prioritize rapid response and safety. These situations often involve fallen trees, broken limbs threatening structures, or partially uprooted trees. The emotional stress of property damage makes clients particularly responsive to companies demonstrating professionalism and calm expertise.

## Discovery of a Diseased or Dying Tree on Property

Visual indicators like leaf discoloration, branch dieback, or fungal growth prompt concern. Clients notice changes in tree appearance during routine property maintenance. These situations trigger online research about symptoms and potential treatments. Clients value educational information that helps them understand the problem and options.

## Tree Branches Threatening Structures or Power Lines

Growing awareness of potential hazards as trees mature near homes or utilities. Clients often notice these issues during seasonal changes when foliage shifts or after minor storms highlight vulnerabilities. Insurance companies may flag these issues during property inspections. Preventive action is motivated by desire to avoid costly damage or power outages.

## Seasonal Cleanup or Property Maintenance Needs

Regular property maintenance schedules prompt consideration of tree services. Spring and fall are particularly active seasons for maintenance inquiries. These services are often planned weeks or months in advance. Clients may bundle multiple tree services for efficiency and cost savings.

## Preparation for Selling Home or Enhancing Curb Appeal

Realtors often recommend tree care as part of pre-listing property preparation. Clients recognize that well-maintained trees significantly impact first impressions. These services are typically time-sensitive to align with market timing. Investment in tree care is viewed as directly enhancing property marketability and value.

## Recommendation from Neighbor Who Recently Used the Service

Word-of-mouth remains a powerful trigger, especially in established neighborhoods. Visible results from neighboring properties create immediate interest. Clients often request "the same service" they observed at a neighbor's property. These referrals come with pre-established trust and expectations.

## Property Insurance Assessment Identifying Hazardous Trees

Insurance inspections may flag specific trees as liabilities. Policy renewals or changes can trigger requirements for tree maintenance. These situations create urgency due to potential policy implications. Documentation of professional service may be required for insurance purposes.

## Research Phase

## Conducts Online Search for Local Tree Services

Google searches for "tree service" plus location are common starting points. Clients investigate company websites for professionalism and service details. Photos of previous work are particularly influential in this phase. Mobile-friendly websites are essential as many searches occur spontaneously while outdoors noticing tree issues.

## Reads Google Business Reviews and Social Media Comments

Star ratings provide initial filtering of service providers. Clients pay particular attention to reviews mentioning safety practices and cleanup quality. Responses to negative reviews are scrutinized for professionalism. Recent reviews (within 6 months) carry more weight than older feedback.

## Asks Neighbors and Community Groups for Recommendations

Neighborhood social media groups serve as trusted recommendation sources. Clients value authentic experiences from people in similar living situations. Multiple mentions of the same company significantly influence consideration. Questions often focus on reliability, pricing transparency, and quality of work.

## Consults with Local Garden Centers or Nurseries

These businesses serve as trusted advisors for plant and tree care. Staff recommendations carry credibility due to perceived expertise.
