# Rugged Tree Service Customer Profile

## Overview of the Typical Client

The typical Rugged Tree Service client falls into several distinct categories, each with specific needs:

Primary Homeowner Client This client typically owns a single-family home on a lot between 0.25-2 acres with 3-12 mature trees. They've owned their property for 5+ years and are beginning to face challenges with tree growth, aging, or seasonal issues. They value their home as both a financial investment and personal sanctuary. While reasonably handy with basic yard maintenance, they recognize tree care as a specialized field requiring professional expertise.

Rural Property Owner This client manages larger parcels (2-100+ acres) with significant wooded areas. They may be facing land management decisions for farming, building expansion, or maintaining natural areas. Their concerns often involve larger-scale projects like clearing storm damage, removing multiple trees, or managing tree lines and windbreaks.

Business/Commercial Client These clients represent commercial properties, homeowners associations, or municipal entities responsible for public spaces. They seek ongoing maintenance relationships to ensure safety and aesthetics while managing liability concerns and budgetary constraints.

Emergency Response Client This situational client reaches out during crisis moments, typically after storms, when trees have damaged structures or created hazardous conditions requiring immediate attention.

## Individual Overview

1. Established Homeowners
* Specific Profile: Residents who have owned their home for 7+ years
* Key Characteristics:
   * Trees have grown considerably during their ownership
   * Facing first major tree maintenance decisions
   * Developing awareness of potential hazards from maturing trees
   * Often triggered to seek services after noticing overhanging branches, roots affecting foundations, or storm damage
* Service Pattern: Typically need combination of trimming and selective removal
2. New Homebuyers
* Specific Profile: Purchased home within last 1-3 years
* Key Characteristics:
   * Often taking on previously neglected tree maintenance
   * Reshaping landscape to match their vision for the property
   * Less familiar with local tree species and their management needs
   * May have limited budget due to recent home purchase expenses
* Service Pattern: Often start with assessment and consultative services
3. Rural Property Owners
* Specific Profile: Manage 5+ acres with significant tree coverage
* Key Characteristics:
   * Need ongoing management of wooded areas
   * Concerned with access roads, field edges, and utility lines
   * Often have equipment to handle some clearing themselves
   * May have agricultural or hunting land considerations
* Service Pattern: Larger scale brush mowing, selective clearing, and management plans
4. Commercial Property Managers
* Specific Profile: Responsible for businesses, churches, or multi-unit housing
* Key Characteristics:
   * Highly concerned with liability and safety
   * Working within defined maintenance budgets
   * Need documentation for insurance and business records
   * Prefer scheduled, predictable service arrangements
* Service Pattern: Annual maintenance contracts with scheduled trimming
5. Senior Citizens
* Specific Profile: Homeowners aged 70+ who can no longer manage yard work
* Key Characteristics:
   * Often long-term residents with substantial emotional attachment to trees
   * Fixed income considerations affecting budget
   * May receive decision support from adult children

6. Post-Storm Emergency Clients
* Specific Profile: Property owners facing immediate hazards from storm damage
* Key Characteristics:
   * Highly stress-driven decision process
   * Time-sensitive needs
   * Often dealing with insurance claims simultaneously
   * May have property access challenges due to debris
* Service Pattern: Require rapid response, documentation for insurance, and complete cleanup

## Decision-Making Process

1. Trigger Events (Problem Recognition)
* Visual Cues: Dead branches, leaning trunks, roots lifting pavement
* Safety Incidents: Near-miss from falling branch, neighbor's tree damage
* Seasonal Transitions: Spring assessment, fall cleanup planning
* Property Changes: New construction requiring tree removal, landscape redesign
* Emergency Situations: Storm damage, lightning strikes, unexpected falls
* Prevention Research: Articles about tree diseases or local insect infestations
2. Research and Information Gathering
* Word-of-Mouth: 65% ask neighbors, friends, or family for recommendations
* Online Research: 58% search for local tree services and read reviews
* Social Media: 42% post questions in community Facebook groups or Nextdoor
* Local Business Directories: 30% consult Chamber of Commerce or local listings
* Home Service Apps: 25% use platforms like Angi or HomeAdvisor
* Previous Service Providers: 15% contact companies they've used for other home services
3. Contact and Initial Assessment
* Contact Methods: Phone calls (65%), website forms (25%), social media messages (10%)
* Information Provided: Brief description of issue, property location, timeline needs
* Response Expectations: 85% expect initial response within 24 hours
* Scheduling Pattern: 70% prefer estimates within a week of contact
4. Evaluation Criteria
* Quote Comparison: Price range and payment terms
* Professionalism Indicators: Promptness, communication clarity, appearance, equipment condition
* Credential Verification: Insurance coverage, certifications, BBB status
* Service Scope: What's included vs. additional costs
* Timeline Commitments: Scheduling flexibility and completion estimates
* Recommendation Quality: Perception of honest assessment vs. upselling
5. Selection Factors (by importance)
* Trust and Rapport: 30% (perception of honesty and competence)
* Price Competitiveness: 25% (not necessarily lowest, but perceived value)
* Availability/Timing: 20% (ability to meet desired schedule)
* Reputation/Reviews: 15% (evidence of quality work)
* Equipment/Capability: 10% (appropriate resources for the job)
6. Scheduling Considerations
* Lead Time Expectations:
   * Routine work: 1-3 weeks acceptable
   * Urgent but non-emergency: Within 1 week
   * Emergencies: Same-day or next-day response
* Scheduling Preferences:
   * 55% prefer weekday scheduling when they can be home
   * 35% are comfortable with weekend appointments
   * 10% request work when they're away (complete trust in provider)
7. Post-Service Evaluation
* Satisfaction Markers:
   * Work quality compared to expectations
   * Cleanup thoroughness
   * Crew professionalism
   * Final price matching estimate
   * Time to completion
* Follow-Up Actions:
   * 30% provide direct feedback
   * 25% write online reviews when prompted
   * 65% would recommend to others if satisfied
   * 45% retain contact information for future needs

## Problems and Pain Points

1. Safety Concerns
* Specific Issues:
   * Dead branches overhanging living spaces, driveways, or play areas
   * Trees with visible decay or structural weakness
   * Storm-damaged trees with hanging limbs ("widow makers")
   * Trees leaning toward structures following ground saturation
   * Root systems compromised by construction or erosion
* Client Thoughts: "That branch looks like it could fall any time... I worry about the kids playing underneath it."
2. Property Damage Risks
* Specific Issues:
   * Roots lifting sidewalks or driveways
   * Branches rubbing against rooflines or siding
   * Roots threatening foundation integrity
   * Tree proximity to utility lines
   * Overcrowded trees creating pressure on structures
* Client Thoughts: "Those roots are starting to crack my driveway, and I'm concerned about what they're doing to my foundation."
3. Aesthetic Challenges
* Specific Issues:
   * Uneven or unbalanced tree growth
   * Overgrown trees blocking desirable views
   * Dead or bare patches in tree canopy
   * Trees incompatible with landscape design
   * Seasonal cleanup from excessive seed pods, fruits, or leaves
* Client Thoughts: "These trees have grown so much they're blocking all the light to my garden."
4. Maintenance Limitations
* Specific Issues:
   * Physical inability to manage pruning at height
   * Lack of proper equipment for larger branches
   * Uncertainty about correct pruning techniques
   * Time constraints preventing regular maintenance
   * Complexity of large tree management
* Client Thoughts: "I used to handle the trimming myself, but these trees have gotten too big for my ladder."
5. Emergency Management
* Specific Issues:
   * Fallen trees blocking access
   * Trees on structures requiring immediate removal
   * Partially fallen trees creating unstable conditions
   * Storm-damaged trees threatening additional structures
   * Cleanup needs exceeding homeowner capacity
* Client Thoughts: "After the storm, this tree is hanging over my garage and I don't know if it's safe to park there."
6. Disease and Pest Concerns
* Specific Issues:
   * Visible signs of insect infestation
   * Fungal growth on trunk or branches
   * Unexplained leaf drop or discoloration
   * Spreading disease affecting multiple trees
   * Emerald ash borer and other regional threats
* Client Thoughts: "I've noticed these spots on the leaves and bare patches appearing... Is this tree sick?"
7. Space Utilization Challenges
* Specific Issues:
   * Trees interfering with construction plans
   * Shade preventing lawn or garden growth
   * Crowded landscape limiting usable outdoor space
   * Trees blocking solar panel efficiency
   * Excessive density creating moisture problems
* Client Thoughts: "We'd like to add a patio here, but these trees are making it impossible."
8. Cleanup Difficulties
* Specific Issues:
   * Seasonal overwhelming of yard waste capacity
   * Inability to manage large branch disposal
   * Stump and root removal after tree cutting
   * Cleaning gutters filled with tree debris
   * Constant leaf or needle accumulation
* Client Thoughts: "Every fall is a battle with leaves from these massive oaks, and it's getting to be too much."
9. Knowledge Gaps
* Specific Issues:
   * Uncertainty about tree health assessment
   * Confusion about proper maintenance timing
   * Inability to identify disease or pest issues
   * Questions about species selection for replacement
   * Lack of understanding about growth patterns
* Client Thoughts: "I don't even know what kind of trees these are, let alone how to care for them properly."
10. Financial Concerns
* Specific Issues:
   * Unexpected costs of emergency tree removal
   * Uncertainty about appropriate pricing
   * Budget constraints vs. necessary work
   * Insurance coverage questions
   * Balancing immediate costs against long-term benefits
* Client Thoughts: "I know this needs to be done, but I'm worried about how much it's going to cost."

## Types of Trees in Southwest Minnesota

1. Native Deciduous Trees

## Bur Oak (Quercus macrocarpa)

* Prevalence: Common throughout region, especially in savannas and open areas
* Characteristics: Massive spreading form, deeply ridged bark, largest acorns of any oak
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 200-400 years, heights of 70-80 feet, spreads of 60-80 feet
* Common Issues: Oak wilt (though more resistant than red oaks), gall-forming insects
* Management Needs: Minimal pruning, avoid root disturbance, prune during dormant season

## Red Oak (Quercus rubra)

* Prevalence: Common in upland forests and residential landscapes
* Characteristics: Rounded crown, distinctive pointed-lobe leaves with bristle tips
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 150-250 years, heights of 60-75 feet, spreads of 45-50 feet
* Common Issues: Highly susceptible to oak wilt, occasional leaf spot diseases
* Management Needs: Avoid pruning April-July (oak wilt risk), maintain mulched root zone

## Sugar Maple (Acer saccharum)

* Prevalence: Common in eastern portions of region, both wild and landscape settings
* Characteristics: Dense oval crown, vibrant fall color, shade-tolerant
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 200-300 years, heights of 60-75 feet, spreads of 40-50 feet
* Common Issues: Susceptible to verticillium wilt, sensitive to salt and compaction
* Management Needs: Protection from soil compaction, maintain consistent moisture

## Silver Maple (Acer saccharinum)

* Prevalence: Very common in residential areas and along waterways
* Characteristics: Fast-growing, brittle wood, distinctive silver underside to leaves
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 80-120 years, heights of 50-70 feet, spreads of 35-50 feet
* Common Issues: Weak wood structure, frequent storm damage, surface roots
* Management Needs: Regular pruning to remove weak branches, monitor near structures

## American Elm (Ulmus americana)

* Prevalence: Historically abundant, now primarily younger specimens or resistant varieties
* Characteristics: Vase-shaped form, doubly serrated leaves, distinctive arching profile
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 80-150 years, heights of 60-80 feet, spreads of 40-60 feet
* Common Issues: Dutch elm disease, elm leaf beetle, elm yellows
* Management Needs: Monitoring for disease symptoms, preventative treatments available

## Basswood/American Linden (Tilia americana)

* Prevalence: Common in richer soils and more sheltered areas
* Characteristics: Dense pyramidal to oval crown, large heart-shaped leaves, fragrant flowers
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 120-200 years, heights of 60-80 feet, spreads of 30-50 feet
* Common Issues: Japanese beetle attraction, occasional leaf spots
* Management Needs: Minimal except for Japanese beetle management in landscapes

## Green Ash (Fraxinus pennsylvanica)

* Prevalence: Historically common but threatened by emerald ash borer
* Characteristics: Compound leaves, adaptable to many soil conditions, moderate growth rate
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 75-120 years, heights of 50-60 feet, spreads of 25-45 feet
* Common Issues: Emerald ash borer (major threat), ash anthracnose, ash decline
* Management Needs: Monitoring for EAB, preventative treatments or planned removal

## Black Walnut (Juglans nigra)

* Prevalence: Scattered throughout region, often in richer bottom lands
* Characteristics: Open rounded crown, compound leaves, produces valuable wood
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 130-250 years, heights of 70-90 feet, spreads of 60-80 feet
* Common Issues: Thousand cankers disease risk, allelopathic to many plants
* Management Needs: Space from gardens (allelopathy), occasional thinning for form

## Cottonwood (Populus deltoides)

* Prevalence: Common along waterways and in wet areas
* Characteristics: Massive size, triangular leaves, fluffy cotton-like seeds
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 70-100 years, heights of 80-100 feet, spreads of 35-60 feet
* Common Issues: Weak wood, aggressive surface roots, messy seed pods
* Management Needs: Not recommended for residential plantings, maintenance pruning near structures

## Paper Birch (Betula papyrifera)

* Prevalence: More common in northern parts of region
* Characteristics: Distinctive white peeling bark, small serrated leaves, golden fall color
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 50-80 years, heights of 50-70 feet, spreads of 30-40 feet
* Common Issues: Bronze birch borer, birch leafminer, heat and drought stress
* Management Needs: Consistent moisture, mulched root zone, protection from stress
2. Native Conifers

## Eastern Red Cedar (Juniperus virginiana)

* Prevalence: Common throughout region, especially in open, dry areas
* Characteristics: Dense pyramidal form, scale-like foliage, blue berry-like cones
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 100-150 years, heights of 40-50 feet, spreads of 10-20 feet
* Common Issues: Cedar-apple rust, bagworms, tends toward interior needle loss
* Management Needs: Minimal; drought-tolerant once established

## White Pine (Pinus strobus)

* Prevalence: More common in eastern portions of region
* Characteristics: Soft blue-green needles in bundles of five, pyramidal when young
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 200-400 years, heights of 80-100 feet, spreads of 25-40 feet
* Common Issues: White pine blister rust, white pine weevil, sensitive to salt
* Management Needs: Protection from winter salt spray, maintain good air circulation

## Red Pine (Pinus resinosa)

* Prevalence: Scattered throughout region, often in plantations
* Characteristics: Reddish bark, needles in pairs, more formal appearance than white pine
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 150-300 years, heights of 60-80 feet, spreads of 25-35 feet
* Common Issues: Diplodia tip blight, pine bark beetles
* Management Needs: Thinning in plantation settings, avoiding root disturbance

## Black Spruce (Picea mariana)

* Prevalence: More common in northern parts of region, especially wet areas
* Characteristics: Narrow spire-like form, short bluish-green needles, small cones
* Typical Age/Size: Lives a long time - up to 200 years, heights of 40-60 feet, very narrow spread
* Common Issues: Spruce budworm, cytospora canker
* Management Needs: Prefers consistently moist, acidic soils

## White Spruce (Picea glauca)

* Prevalence: More common in northern parts of region
* Characteristics: Pyramidal form, bluish-green needles, larger than black spruce
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 150-300 years, heights of 50-70 feet, spreads of 15-25 feet
* Common Issues: Spruce budworm, spider mites, needle cast diseases
* Management Needs: Prefers cool sites with consistent moisture
3. Common Landscape Trees

## Colorado Blue Spruce (Picea pungens)

* Prevalence: Very common in landscapes throughout region
* Characteristics: Distinctive blue-silver needles, formal pyramidal form
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 80-120 years, heights of 50-70 feet, spreads of 15-25 feet
* Common Issues: Cytospora canker, needle cast diseases, spruce gall adelgid
* Management Needs: Good air circulation, avoid overhead watering

## Norway Maple (Acer platanoides)

* Prevalence: Common in older neighborhoods and parks

* Typical Age/Size: Lives 80-150 years, heights of 50-60 feet, spreads of 40-50 feet
* Common Issues: Surface roots, verticillium wilt, invasive potential
* Management Needs: Monitoring for seedling spread, occasional pruning for structure

## Honey Locust (Gleditsia triacanthos var. inermis)

* Prevalence: Common street and landscape tree throughout region
* Characteristics: Thornless varieties with fine textured foliage, filtered shade
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 100-120 years, heights of 40-70 feet, spreads of 30-50 feet
* Common Issues: Pod gall midge, webworm, occasional leaf spot diseases
* Management Needs: Minimal; adaptable and generally trouble-free

## American Linden/Basswood (Tilia americana)

* Prevalence: Common shade tree in parks and larger properties
* Characteristics: Pyramidal to oval form, large heart-shaped leaves, fragrant flowers
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 120-200 years, heights of 60-80 feet, spreads of 30-50 feet
* Common Issues: Japanese beetle attraction, occasional leaf spots
* Management Needs: Minimal except for Japanese beetle management

## Crabapple (Malus varieties)

* Prevalence: Extremely common ornamental tree throughout region
* Characteristics: Spring flowers, varieties with different sizes, forms, fruit colors
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 30-70 years, heights of 15-25 feet, similar spreads
* Common Issues: Apple scab, fireblight, cedar-apple rust
* Management Needs: Disease-resistant varieties recommended, pruning for structure

## Mountain Ash (Sorbus aucuparia)

* Prevalence: Occasional ornamental tree in landscapes
* Characteristics: White flower clusters, orange-red berries, compound leaves
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 30-50 years, heights of 20-35 feet, spreads of 15-25 feet
* Common Issues: Fireblight, ash borer, mountain ash sawfly
* Management Needs: Good air circulation, consistent moisture, prompt disease management

## River Birch (Betula nigra)

* Prevalence: Increasingly common landscape tree replacing paper birch
* Characteristics: Exfoliating salmon-brown bark, more heat tolerant than paper birch
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 50-100 years, heights of 40-70 feet, spreads of 30-50 feet
* Common Issues: Iron chlorosis in alkaline soils, more resistant to bronze birch borer
* Management Needs: Consistent moisture, mulched root zone

## Serviceberry (Amelanchier species)

* Prevalence: Common small ornamental tree/large shrub in landscapes
* Characteristics: Early white flowers, edible berries, excellent fall color
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 40-80 years, heights of 15-25 feet, spreads of 10-20 feet
* Common Issues: Cedar-quince rust, occasional leaf spots, generally trouble-free
* Management Needs: Minimal; occasional pruning for form
4. Fruit Trees

## Apple (Malus domestica varieties)

* Prevalence: Common in home landscapes and remnant orchards
* Characteristics: Spring flowers, fall fruit, variety-dependent size and form
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 30-60 years, heights of 15-25 feet, similar spreads
* Common Issues: Apple scab, fireblight, codling moth, apple maggot
* Management Needs: Annual pruning, pest management for fruit production

## Plum (Prunus varieties)

* Prevalence: Both American and European varieties in landscapes
* Characteristics: Early spring flowers, summer fruit, rounded form
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 30-50 years, heights of 15-20 feet, similar spreads
* Common Issues: Black knot, plum curculio, brown rot
* Management Needs: Annual pruning, disease monitoring, fruit thinning

## Cherry (Prunus varieties)

* Prevalence: Both ornamental and fruiting types in landscapes
* Characteristics: Abundant spring flowers, some with edible fruit
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 20-40 years, heights of 15-30 feet depending on variety
* Common Issues: Cherry leaf spot, black knot, borers
* Management Needs: Pruning for air circulation, disease management

## Pear (Pyrus communis - hardy varieties)

* Prevalence: Occasional in home landscapes
* Characteristics: White flowers, upright pyramidal form, fall fruit
* Typical Age/Size: Lives 35-65 years, heights of 20-30 feet, narrower spreads
* Common Issues: Fireblight (major concern), pear psylla, pear scab
* Management Needs: Careful pruning to prevent fireblight spread, variety selection
5. Common Regional Tree Issues

## Dutch Elm Disease

* Causal Agent: Ophiostoma ulmi and O. novo-ulmi fungi
* Transmission: Bark beetles, root grafts between adjacent trees
* Symptoms: Wilting and yellowing of leaves on individual branches, progressing to entire crown
* Impact: Has killed millions of American elms; continues to affect remaining large specimens
* Management: Resistant varieties, preventative fungicide treatments, prompt removal of infected trees

## Emerald Ash Borer

* Causal Agent: Agrilus planipennis (invasive beetle)
* Transmission: Adult flight, movement of infested wood
* Symptoms: Canopy thinning from top down, D-shaped exit holes, bark splitting, epicormic shoots
* Impact: Fatal to all native ash species; spreading throughout region
* Management: Preventative insecticide treatments, planned removal and replacement

## Oak Wilt

* Causal Agent: Bretziella fagacearum fungus
* Transmission: Sap-feeding beetles, root grafts between adjacent oaks
* Symptoms: Rapid wilting from top down, leaf drop, vascular discoloration (red oaks more susceptible)
* Impact: Often fatal, especially to red oak group; white oaks may recover
* Management: Avoid pruning during high-risk season (April-July), root graft disruption, prompt removal

## Storm Damage

* Causal Factors: High winds, ice accumulation, heavy snow, lightning
* Vulnerable Trees: Those with structural defects, included bark, co-dominant stems
* Common Damage: Branch breakage, crown loss, uprooting, trunk splitting
* Impact: Immediate safety hazards, entry points for decay organisms
* Management: Preventative structural pruning, cable/bracing systems, prompt professional repair

## Winter Injury

* Causal Factors: Extreme cold, freeze-thaw cycles, winter desiccation
* Vulnerable Trees: Young or newly planted trees, marginally hardy species
* Common Damage: Bark splitting, twig dieback, sunscald on southwest sides
* Impact: Reduced vigor, secondary pest susceptibility, occasional mortality
* Management: Proper species selection, trunk wrapping of young trees, adequate fall moisture

## Drought Stress

* Causal Factors: Extended periods without rainfall, high temperatures
* Vulnerable Trees: Newly planted specimens, shallow-rooted species
* Common Damage: Leaf scorch, premature leaf drop, twig dieback, reduced growth
* Impact: Increased susceptibility to secondary pests and diseases
* Management: Supplemental watering, proper mulching practices, drought-tolerant species selection
