## Types of Trees in that Area

1. Native Deciduous Trees
* Oak Species:
   * White Oak (Quercus alba): Long-lived, broad specimens common in older neighborhoods
   * Red Oak (Quercus rubra): Faster growing, found in developments from 1960s-1990s
   * Bur Oak (Quercus macrocarpa): Massive specimens in historic districts
   * Pin Oak (Quercus palustris): Common in wetter areas and newer developments
   * Common issues: Oak wilt, bacterial leaf scorch, gall-forming insects
   * Maintenance needs: Structural pruning when young, deadwood removal in maturity
* Maple Species:
   * Silver Maple (Acer saccharinum): Fast-growing, weak-wooded, common in post-war neighborhoods
   * Sugar Maple (Acer saccharum): Slower growing, fall color specimen trees
   * Red Maple (Acer rubrum): Increasingly common in newer developments
   * Common issues: Verticillium wilt, leaf scorch, gloomy scale, weak branch attachments
   * Maintenance needs: Regular structural pruning, frequent deadwood management
* Other Significant Natives:
   * American Elm (Ulmus americana): Surviving specimens or resistant varieties
   * Ash (Fraxinus spp.): Declining due to Emerald Ash Borer infestation
   * Hickory (Carya spp.): Found in older properties and natural areas
   * Sycamore (Platanus occidentalis): Large specimens near water features
   * River Birch (Betula nigra): Popular multi-stemmed landscape tree
   * Common issues: Species-specific diseases, storm damage susceptibility
   * Maintenance needs: Regular monitoring, preventative pruning
2. Ornamental and Landscape Trees
* Flowering Specimen Trees:
   * Dogwood (Cornus florida): Understory tree with spring flowers
   * Redbud (Cercis canadensis): Native understory tree with purple spring blooms
   * Crabapple (Malus spp.): Common in landscapes, multiple varieties
   * Japanese Maple (Acer palmatum): High-value specimen trees in protected locations
   * Serviceberry (Amelanchier spp.): Multi-season interest, becoming more popular
   * Common issues: Apple scab (crabapple), anthracnose (dogwood), environmental stress
   * Maintenance needs: Light pruning, disease monitoring, structure development
* Evergreen Landscape Trees:
   * White Pine (Pinus strobus): Tall specimens in older properties
   * Norway Spruce (Picea abies): Common windbreak and specimen tree
   * Colorado Blue Spruce (Picea pungens): Distinctive color, often in front yards
   * Arborvitae (Thuja occidentalis): Common for screening, various cultivars
   * Juniper (Juniperus spp.): Drought-tolerant landscapes, various forms
   * Common issues: Needle cast diseases, bagworms, winter burn, drought stress
   * Maintenance needs: Minimal pruning, pest monitoring, mulching
* Non-Native Deciduous Trees:
   * Bradford/Callery Pear (Pyrus calleryana): Common in commercial landscapes
   * Norway Maple (Acer platanoides): Found in older neighborhoods
   * Ginkgo (Ginkgo biloba): Street tree and specimen plantings
   * London Planetree (Platanus x acerifolia): Urban-tolerant large shade tree
   * Honey Locust (Gleditsia triacanthos): Common in newer developments
   * Common issues: Structural weaknesses, invasive potential, pest susceptibility
   * Maintenance needs: Structural pruning, containment, monitoring
3. Common Tree Problems in the Area
* Regional Disease Challenges:
   * Emerald Ash Borer: Devastating ash populations throughout region
   * Oak Wilt: Spreading threat to valuable oak specimens
   * Dutch Elm Disease: Continuing threat to surviving elms
   * Apple Scab: Affecting crabapples throughout the area
   * Verticillium Wilt: Affecting maples and other species
   * Management approaches: Preventative treatments, proper pruning timing, monitoring
* Environmental Stress Factors:
   * Urban soil compaction: Common in older neighborhoods
   * Construction damage: Prevalent in developing areas
   * Road salt exposure: Street trees and roadside specimens
   * Drought cycles: Increasingly common with climate patterns
   * Heat stress: Urban heat island effects in developed areas
   * Management approaches: Mulching, proper watering, soil management
* Weather-Related Challenges:
   * Severe thunderstorm damage: Frequent summer occurrence
   * Ice storm loading: Winter branch failures
   * Wind shear: Tornado-prone region with sudden gusts
   * Lightning strikes: Tall trees in open areas
