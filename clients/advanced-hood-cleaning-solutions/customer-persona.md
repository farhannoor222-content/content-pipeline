# Advanced Hood Cleaning Solutions Customer Profile

## Overview of the Typical Client

The typical client of Advanced Hood Cleaning Solutions is a restaurant owner or kitchen manager operating in Buffalo, Rochester, or Syracuse, NY. They run establishments ranging from casual dining to upscale restaurants where commercial kitchen equipment is heavily used daily. These clients typically have 5-15 years of experience in the food service industry and are responsible for both the operational and safety aspects of their kitchens. They often struggle balancing daily business operations with regulatory compliance requirements. These clients usually manage kitchen staff of 5-20 employees and are directly accountable for health and safety compliance. Most have experienced at least one serious grease buildup issue or have been cited during an inspection in the past.

## Demographics

* Age: Primarily 35-55 years old, with newer restaurant owners in their early 30s and established owners up to 60
* Gender: Approximately 65% male, 35% female, reflecting current industry ownership patterns in upstate New York
* Income: Restaurant owners earning $80,000-$500,000 annually depending on establishment size and success; kitchen managers typically earning $45,000-$75,000
* Education: 40% have culinary degrees, 30% have business degrees, and 30% have high school education with practical experience
* Location: Concentrated in urban and suburban areas within a 50-mile radius of Buffalo, Rochester, and Syracuse, with some in smaller towns throughout the service area
* Occupation: 60% independent restaurant owners, 25% corporate kitchen managers, 15% institutional food service directors (schools, hospitals, catering businesses)
* Housing: 70% homeowners, typically living within 20 minutes of their business
* Family Status: 65% married with children, often working long hours and balancing family responsibilities with demanding business schedules

## Demographic Example: Meet Mike

Mike Romano is a 42-year-old restaurant owner who runs "Romano's Italian Kitchen," a busy 80-seat restaurant in Rochester's arts district. He started as a line cook at 18, worked his way up, and opened his first restaurant at 32. Ten years later, he owns two locations and employs 28 people across both restaurants.

Mike holds an associate's degree in culinary arts from the Culinary Institute of America and learned business management through experience and night classes. He earns approximately $120,000 annually but reinvests much of his profit into his restaurants. He lives in a $375,000 home in Brighton, a suburb just outside Rochester, with his wife Maria who handles the restaurant's bookkeeping and their two teenagers.

Mike works 60+ hours weekly, dividing time between locations, and relies on trusted kitchen managers. He's been cited once for hood system non-compliance three years ago, which cost him $2,500 in fines and repairs. Since then, he's become vigilant about exhaust system maintenance but struggles keeping up with changing regulations. His primary concerns are fire safety, staff reliability, and maintaining food quality while managing costs.

## Decision-Making Process

1. Initial Trigger Events:
   * Upcoming health department or fire marshal inspection (typically scheduled annually)
   * Visual indication of grease buildup in visible parts of the system
   * Insurance company requirement for professional cleaning documentation
   * System malfunction (poor ventilation, smoking hood, strange odors)
   * Change in ownership or management requiring system assessment
   * Recommendation from equipment repair technician
   * Previous citation or fine for non-compliance
2. Research Phase:
   * Google searches using terms like "commercial hood cleaning near me," "NFPA 96 compliant hood service," "restaurant exhaust cleaning"
   * Asking fellow restaurant owners at local industry events or through social media groups
   * Consulting with their fire suppression system service provider
   * Reviewing service providers' websites for expertise signals and photo evidence
   * Checking online reviews on Google Business, Yelp, and industry forums
   * Verifying NFPA 96 knowledge through initial phone conversations
   * Assessing response time to inquiry as indication of service quality
3. Evaluation Criteria:
   * Depth of knowledge about NFPA 96 requirements and FDA standards
   * Credentials, certifications, and years in business
   * Specific experience with their type of kitchen and exhaust system
   * Quality of reference photos from previous jobs
   * Transparency in pricing and service details
   * Flexibility in scheduling to minimize business disruption
   * Reputation from peer references and online reviews
   * Professionalism in initial consultation
   * Understanding of insurance documentation requirements
4. Selection Process:
   * Typically requests 2-3 quotes before deciding
   * Values providers who perform thorough on-site assessment before quoting
   * Prefers companies that explain what they'll do rather than just giving a price
   * Often makes final decision based on perceived expertise rather than lowest price
   * Considers scheduling flexibility crucial during busy seasons
   * Weighs the comprehensiveness of the service and documentation offered
5. Retention Factors:
   * Thoroughness of cleaning (especially in hard-to-reach areas)
   * Quality and detail of before/after documentation
   * Professionalism of technicians and minimal disruption
   * Clear explanation of any compliance issues found
   * Consistency in service quality over multiple visits
   * Proactive reminders for scheduled maintenance
   * Relationship building and familiarity with their specific system
