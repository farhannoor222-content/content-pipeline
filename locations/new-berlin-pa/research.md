# Location Research: New Berlin, PA

Researched 2026-11-01 for Kerlin Tree Service. Facts come from web search results; source pages could not be opened from the research session, so confirm anything critical before reuse.

## Population and Tier

- 802 (2020 Census; the borough's own site says 801)
- Tier 1 (under 5,000)
- Borough in Union County, on the north bank of Penns Creek. The creek's south bank is the Union and Snyder county line.

## Nickname and Local Terms

- No nickname found. Area was known as Longstown in 1782; permanent settlement on Penns Creek dates to 1792 (George Long).
- New Berlin Day: street festival held each year at the end of August.

## Tree Regulations

- No borough tree or shade tree ordinance found in search results. Not confirmed either way; check with the borough office before writing permit content.
- Spotted lanternfly: Union County joined Pennsylvania's spotted lanternfly quarantine in March 2022. Businesses operating in or traveling through quarantine areas need a Spotted Lanternfly permit.

## Local Tree Problems and Climate

- Penns Creek flooding: the NWS gauge at Penns Creek (village, downstream) lists flood stage 8 ft, 78 recorded floods, last flood 12/22/2018. Record crest from the 1933 hurricane. That gauge is not in New Berlin itself.
- Spotted lanternfly (county quarantine, see above)
- Region-wide from customer-persona.md: emerald ash borer, heavy wet snow and ice, spring thunderstorms, sycamore anthracnose near river valleys, windthrow on pines and spruce.
- Soil: creekside floodplain soil along Penns Creek (no borough-specific soil survey found)

## Neighborhoods and Landmarks

- Market Street: main street. Former jail at 427-429 Market St. is now residential.
- New Berlin Heritage Museum: in the original Union County Courthouse (built 1815) at Market and Vine Streets. Also houses the post office. Open by appointment and on New Berlin Day.
- Penns Creek: south edge of the borough, county line.
- Vine Street: meets Market Street at the old courthouse.
- Bridge over Penns Creek: a newer bridge replaced the old one; the old bridge's swimming rope was retired to the museum. Bridge name not confirmed.
- Conflict: one source calls New Berlin the original county seat, another says Mifflinburg was first. Not used.
