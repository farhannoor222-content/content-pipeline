# Location Research: Rutland City, VT

Researched 2026-11-01 for Iconic Tree Care. Facts come from web search results. The source pages themselves could not be opened from the research session, so confirm anything critical before reuse.

## Population and Tier

- 15,807 (2020 Census, U.S. Census QuickFacts)
- Tier 2 (5,000 to 20,000)
- Third-largest city in Vermont. Rutland City is surrounded by Rutland Town, which is a separate municipality with its own rules.

## Tree Regulations

- Rutland City Tree Ordinance (Chapter 6, copy dated 2018): no one may cut, climb, trim, remove, or injure trees on public rights of way or other city property without a permit from the commissioner of public works or the city forester and arborist.
- The commissioner of public works serves as tree warden. The city forester and arborist manages the city's street trees.
- The city can require removal of a tree on public or private land that the forester finds is a danger to public safety, and bill the owner if they do not act.
- Digging or regrading in a public right of way needs a separate "digging in the street permit."
- Not confirmed: whether removing a tree entirely on private property needs a permit. Contact Rutland City DPW, Forester and Arborist division.
- Do not mix up with the Village of Rutland (a different municipality with its own Chapter 154 tree rules).

## Local Tree Problems

- Emerald ash borer: first confirmed in Rutland County (West Rutland) in October 2020. A September 2025 city announcement reported it found in the North Main Street cemetery in the city.
- City response: removed 235 public ash trees in 2018 to 2019 and began treating 120 street ash trees with emamectin benzoate in 2020.
- Flooding: July 2023 flash floods hit the southern part of the city (at least 26 residents displaced per the fire chief). Further regional flooding in December 2023 and July 2024. Otter Creek flood levels in 2023 exceeded Tropical Storm Irene in 2011.

## Neighborhoods and Landmarks

- Downtown Rutland: Center Street is the core of downtown. Rutland Downtown Historic District.
- Paramount Theatre: 30 Center Street, opened 1913, restored and reopened March 2000 as a performance venue.
- Pine Hill Park: northwest corner of the city, about 300 to 325 acres, 16 to 18 miles of single-track trail (sources vary). Run by the volunteer Pine Hill Partnership.
- Giorgetti Park and Athletic Complex: next to Pine Hill Park, ice rink, turf, Flip Side Skatepark under an open-sided roof.
- Main Street Park: town green with a gazebo that hosts the farmers market.
- Depot Park: former railroad station site near the current Amtrak station.
- Evergreen Cemetery (465 West Street, est. 1861): sources disagree whether it is in Rutland City or Rutland Town. Not used.
